Manny Rutinel defeats incumbent Gabe Evans in battleground 8th CD

By Ernest Luning
Published November 3, 2026 7:45pm |Updated November 5, 2026 2:03pm
+ Add Colorado Politics on Google

Democrat Manny Rutinel defeated Republican U.S. Rep. Gabe Evans Tuesday in Colorado’s battleground 8th Congressional District by a double-digit margin in what was expected to be a tight race.

In updated results posted by 1:30 p.m. Thursday, Rutinel had 55% of the vote to Evans’ 42%. Libertarian Dave Wood had just under 4%.

After the Associated Press called the race late Tuesday, Rutinel said, “Tonight, Colorado showed that in America, the son of a single mom who relied on Medicaid and food stamps can go on to serve in Congress. This election was decided at kitchen tables, where families are weighing the cost of groceries, gas, and healthcare.”

“Tonight, Coloradans decided that kids shouldn’t go hungry, that families deserve healthcare, and that neither a trillionaire nor the President of the United States could buy this election,” he added.

The nearly evenly divided district, which covers parts of Adams, Weld and Larimer counties north of the Denver metro area, has been considered Colorado’s most competitive House seat since its creation after the 2020 census.

The 8th CD sent one Democrat and one Republican to Congress before this election, with each contest decided by less than 1% of the vote.

Both major parties again targeted the district as soon as the 2026 election cycle got underway. Outside spending in the district this cycle hovering around $40 million, making it one of this year’s most expensive congressional elections in the country — and one of the 10 most expensive House races on record.

While the race was rated a “toss-up” by national election forecasters until late October, the major nonpartisan prognosticators all moved it in the Democrats’ favor in the week before the election, citing what Inside Elections called “a looming wipeout at the top of the ticket for Republicans” and a swing from GOP candidates by Hispanic voters.

The 8th CD was drawn to be the state’s “Hispanic influence” district, with the group making up an estimated 40% of its population. Both major party nominees are Hispanic — Evans is the descendant of Mexican immigrants and Rutinel is the son of a single mother who immigrated from the Dominican Republic.

Evans, a 40-year-old U.S. Army and Colorado Army National Guard veteran and former Arvada police officer from Fort Lupton, spent one term in the Colorado House before defeating Democratic U.S. Rep. Yadira Caraveo in the 2024 election by less than 2,500 votes, a margin of less than 1 percentage point. At the same time, President Donald Trump carried the district by just under 2 points.

Rutinel, 31, is a former state representative from Commerce City. He resigned from the legislature after winning the nomination by defeating former state Rep. Shannon Bird by a 30-point margin in the primary.

Rutinel, who has a graduate degree in applied economics from Johns Hopkins and a law degree from Yale, worked as an environmental attorney for environmental group Earthjustice and as an economist for the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.

The candidates both campaigned with a parade of heavy-hitters from their respective parties in the closing weeks, with House Speaker Mike Johnson making appearances for Evans and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries and likely 2028 presidential candidate Pete Buttigieg showing up for Rutinel.

Although Evans led in fundraising through most of the cycle, Rutinel passed the incumbent in the most recent quarter with a record-setting $4.2 million in receipts, bringing the Democrat’s total to nearly $9.5 million through Oct. 14. Evans reported raising more than $7.2 million over the same stretch.
