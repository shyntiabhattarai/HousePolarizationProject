The U.S. House of Representatives elections in Michigan were on November 3, 2026. Voters elected 13 candidates to serve in the U.S. House from each of the state's 13 U.S. House districts. The primary was August 4, 2026. The filing deadline was April 21, 2026.

                            General election race ratings

                            See also: Race rating definitions and methods

                            Ballotpedia provides race ratings from four outlets: The Cook Political Report, Inside Elections, Sabato's Crystal Ball, and DDHQ/The Hill. Each race rating indicates if one party is perceived to have an advantage in the race and, if so, the degree of advantage:

                            • Safe and Solid ratings indicate that one party has a clear edge and the race is not competitive.
                            • Likely ratings indicate that one party has a clear edge, but an upset is possible.
                            • Lean ratings indicate that one party has a small edge, but the race is competitive.[1]
                            • Toss-up ratings indicate that neither party has an advantage.

                            Race ratings are informed by a number of factors, including polling, candidate quality, and election result history in the race's district or state.[2][3][4]

                            Post-filing deadline analysis

                            The following analysis covers all U.S. House districts up for election in Michigan in 2026. Information below was calculated on April 21, 2026, and may differ from information shown in the table above due to candidate replacements and withdrawals after that time.

                            The 2026 election cycle had the fourth-highest number of candidates who ran for the U.S. House of Representatives since 2014. Fifty candidates — 28 Democrats and 22 Republicans — ran for Michigan’s 13 congressional districts. That’s 3.9 candidates per district. There were 3.3 candidates per district in 2024, 4.1 in 2022, 3.9 in 2020, 3.6 in 2018, 2.7 in 2016, and 3.4 in 2014. Michigan had 13 congressional districts in the 2026, 2024, and 2022 election cycles. It had 14 congressional districts in all other cycles from 2014 to 2020.

                            In total, 13 primaries — eight Democrats and five Republicans — were contested in 2026. In total, there were 12 contested primaries in 2024, 13 in 2022, 15 in 2020, 10 in 2018, six in 2016, and 13 in 2014.

                            Incumbents — two Democrats and two Republicans — ran in four of those primaries. There were five incumbents in contested primaries in 2024, seven in 2022, four in 2020, one in 2018, three in 2016, and six in 2014.

                            Two districts were open in 2026 because the districts’ incumbents both retired to run for other offices. Rep. Haley Stevens (D-11th) ran for the U.S. Senate, and Rep. John James (R-10th) ran for governor of Michigan. There were two open districts in 2024, two in 2022, one in 2020, two in 2018, two in 2016, and four in 2014.

                            The open 10th Congressional District attracted the most candidates. Seven candidates — three Democrats and four Republicans — ran for the district.

                            Candidates filed to run in the Republican and Democratic primaries in all 13 districts, meaning no districts were guaranteed to either party.
