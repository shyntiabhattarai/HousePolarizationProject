Heading into the November 3, 2026, election, all 435 seats in the U.S. House of Representatives were up for election. Five of the chamber’s six non-voting members were also up for election.

Republicans held a 218-214 majority in the U.S. House, with one independent who caucuses with Republicans and two vacancies. Democrats were defending 22 districts that Donald Trump (R) won in the 2024 presidential election. Republicans were defending eight districts that Kamala Harris (D) won in 2024.

Ballotpedia identified 50 races (11.5%) as general election battlegrounds in 2026. Democrats held 27 of those seats, and Republicans held 23.

Nine states used different congressional maps than they did in 2024. Five states voluntarily redrew their maps mid-decade: California, Florida, North Carolina, Tennessee, and Texas. Ohio adopted a new map because of state law. Alabama, Louisiana, and Utah adopted new maps because of litigation.

Sixty incumbents did not seek re-election. Thirty-two incumbents retired from public office, and 28 ran for other offices. Click here to learn more.

Ballotpedia does not include incumbents who leave office early or lose re-election in a primary in our analysis of incumbents not running for re-election. Eleven House incumbents have lost re-election in primaries this year. For more information about incumbents who left office early, announced resignations, or lost primaries, click here.

What we’re watching

Below are the storylines we're watching in the 2026 U.S. House elections.

  • Which party will control the U.S. House after the elections? Republicans hold 218-214 majority in the U.S. House. One member is an independent who caucuses with Republicans, and two seats are vacant. Republicans won control of the U.S. House as a result of the 2024 elections.
  • How many U.S. House seats will change party hands? In 2024, Democrats won eight Republican-held seats, and Republicans won nine Democratic-held seats.
  • How many members of the U.S. House will lose re-election? From 2012 to 2024, an average of approximately 22 U.S. Representatives lost their re-election bids. A total of 85 U.S. Representatives lost re-election in general elections during that period, and 40 lost re-election in a primary election.
  • How will each party's 2026 results in congressional districts that held special elections for the 119th Congress compare with the special election results? Eleven U.S. House special elections were held during the 119th Congress, six for Democratic-held seats and five for Republican-held seats. No seat changed party hands. In seven of those districts, the Democratic candidate’s margin was better than the Democratic presidential candidate’s 2024 margin.
  • How many open U.S. House seats will change party hands? Sixty U.S. Representatives are not running for re-election in 2026. Heading into the elections, 23 of them are Democrats and 37 are Republicans.
  • How many U.S. House members will win in districts that the other party's presidential candidate carried in 2024? Democrats are defending 22 districts that Donald Trump (R) won in the 2024 presidential election. Republicans are defending eight districts that Kamala Harris (D) won in 2024.
  • Which party will win districts re-drawn during mid-decade redistricting? Between the 2024 and 2026 elections, six states enacted new Congressional district maps that, on net, were intended to give Republicans nine additional Congressional districts.
  • How many U.S. House seats will Republicans gain or lose nationwide during a mid-term election with a Republican president? In elections from 1918-2016, the median number of seats the president's party lost was six. The average was 14. For 2026 to qualify as a wave election according to Ballotpedia's research, Republicans would need to lose 48 seats for the elections to qualify as a Democratic wave.
  • In U.S. House races that repeat a 2024 matchup, how many winners of the 2024 election will win again, and how many 2024 runners-up will win? Forty-four U.S. House races in 2026 are rematches of 2024 contests, or 10.1% of all House races. In 2024, Democrats won 23 of these matchups and Republicans won 21. Ten of the 44 were decided by 10 percentage points or fewer in 2024. Democrats won six of those 10 races and Republicans won four.
