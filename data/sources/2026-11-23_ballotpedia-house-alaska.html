U.S. House elections, 2026

The U.S. House of Representatives election in Alaska was on November 3, 2026. Voters elected one candidate to serve in the U.S. House from the state's one at-large U.S. House district. The primary was August 18, 2026. The filing deadline was June 1, 2026. The outcome of this race affected the partisan balance of the U.S. House of Representatives in the 120th Congress. All 435 U.S. House districts were up for election.

At the time of the election, Republicans had a 219-214 majority with two vacancies in the U.S. House.[1][2] To read more about the U.S. House elections that took place in 2026, click here.

Satellite spending

Satellite spending describes political spending not controlled by candidates or their campaigns; that is, any political expenditures made by groups or individuals that are not directly affiliated with a candidate. This includes spending by political party committees, super PACs, trade associations, and 501(c)(4) nonprofit groups.[5][6]

General election race ratings

See also: Race rating definitions and methods

Ballotpedia provides race ratings from four outlets: The Cook Political Report, Inside Elections, Sabato's Crystal Ball, and DDHQ/The Hill. Each race rating indicates if one party is perceived to have an advantage in the race and, if so, the degree of advantage:

  • Safe and Solid ratings indicate that one party has a clear edge and the race is not competitive.
  • Likely ratings indicate that one party has a clear edge, but an upset is possible.
  • Lean ratings indicate that one party has a small edge, but the race is competitive.[7]
  • Toss-up ratings indicate that neither party has an advantage.

Race ratings are informed by a number of factors, including polling, candidate quality, and election result history in the race's district or state.[8][9][10]

Prediction markets

See also: Prediction markets

A prediction market is a platform where users buy and sell contracts tied to the outcome of future events. The price of a contract reflects the market's estimated probability of that outcome — a contract priced at 55 cents suggests the market estimates a 55 percent chance the event occurs. Volume reflects how actively a market is being traded.[11] Unlike polls, which survey a sample of voters, prediction markets aggregate the financial stakes of individual bettors. A market with higher trading volume is generally considered more reliable than one with low volume, though prices can be influenced by large individual traders.

Post-filing deadline analysis

The following analysis covers all U.S. House districts up for election in Alaska in 2026. Information below was calculated on June 1, 2026, and may differ from information shown in the table above due to candidate replacements and withdrawals after that time.

The 2026 election cycle was the third year that the election for Alaska's At-Large Congressional District used a top-four primary system. In a top-four primary, candidates run in a single primary election, regardless of party affiliation. The four candidates who receive the most votes advance to the general election. In the general election, voters use ranked-choice voting (RCV) to select the winner.

The 2026 election cycle had the second-highest number of candidates who ran for the district since 2014. Fifteen candidates — four Democrats, three Republicans, two Libertarians, five nonpartisan, and one undeclared — ran for the district. Twelve candidates ran in 2024, 22 ran in 2022, six ran in 2020, seven ran in 2018, seven ran in 2016, and six ran in 2014.

Among the candidates was Rep. Nicholas Begich (R). As a result, the district was not open in 2026. An incumbent ran for re-election in the district every year since 2014.

Begich ran in a contested primary in 2026. An incumbent ran in a contested primary for the district every year since 2014.

Democratic and Republican candidates filed to run in the primary, meaning the district was not guaranteed to either party.
