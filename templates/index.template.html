Hollowing the Middle? The 2026 Wave and House Polarization

Research question: Did the November 3, 2026 U.S. House elections disproportionately remove moderate Republicans? And among 119th-Congress House members who will return in the 120th, does the gap between party means on DW-NOMINATE dimension 1 mechanically widen?

{{HEADLINE}}
Read this first.
  • This covers 119th-Congress members who will return. Newly elected members have no scores yet, so this is not a forecast of the 120th Congress.
  • Races that were still pending are excluded from the main result.
  • Results are unofficial as of the source access dates ({{ACCESS_DATES}}).
  • Members who left the House before Nov 3 are excluded (decision 1).

What is DW-NOMINATE?

This section explains the score used on every chart below.

DW-NOMINATE is a score that places each member of Congress on a left–right line using only how they vote on roll calls. Members who vote together get similar scores; negative scores are more liberal and positive scores are more conservative.

Were the losses unusually moderate?

This asks whether the Republicans who lost their general elections were more moderate than a random group of Republicans would be.

If voters had removed Republicans at random, how often would a random group of the same size be as moderate as the ones who actually lost? The computer drew {{N_DRAWS}} random groups of Republicans; the p-value is the share of groups that were at least as moderate, so a small p-value means the real losses were unusually moderate.
